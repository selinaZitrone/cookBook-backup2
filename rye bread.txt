**Not That Simple Caraway Rye Bread**

This recipe blends rye flour with all-purpose flour to ensure the bread rises beautifully while maintaining that distinct, deep rye flavor.

Overview

+ Prep Time: 1 hour 15 minutes (mostly rising time)
+ Cook Time: 30 minutes
+ Yield: 1 round loaf (approx. 8 slices)

Ingredients

+ 2 cups rye flour
+ 1 cup all-purpose flour (plus extra for dusting)
+ 1 packet (7g or 2¼ tsp) active dry yeast
+ 1.5 cups warm water (about 105°F–110°F)
+ 1 tbsp sugar
+ 1 tbsp caraway seeds (optional, for that classic deli flavor)
+ 1 tsp salt

Instructions

1. Activate the yeast: In a small bowl, dissolve the sugar and active dry yeast in the warm water. Let it sit for about 5 minutes until it becomes frothy.
2. Mix the dry ingredients: In a large mixing bowl, combine the rye flour, all-purpose flour, salt, and caraway seeds (if using).
3. Form the dough: Pour the frothy yeast mixture into the dry ingredients. Stir with a wooden spoon or your hands until a cohesive dough forms.
4. Knead: Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Knead for about 10 minutes until it is smooth and elastic. (Note: Rye dough is naturally stickier than wheat dough, so avoid adding too much extra flour).
5. First rise: Place the dough in a lightly greased bowl, cover it with a damp cloth or plastic wrap, and let it rise in a warm place for 1 hour, or until it doubles in size.
6. Shape: Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Punch down the risen dough to remove air bubbles, shape it into an oblong or round loaf, and place it onto a baking sheet.
7. Bake: Bake for 30 minutes. You will know it is done when the crust is golden brown and the loaf sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom.
8. Cool: Transfer the loaf to a wire rack and let it cool completely before slicing.

Pro Tips

+ The Gluten Balance: Pure rye flour has very little gluten, which means 100% rye loaves can turn out as dense as bricks. Mixing in all-purpose or bread flour gives the dough the structure it needs to rise.
+ For a Crispy Crust: Toss a few ice cubes into a shallow metal pan on the bottom rack of your oven right as you put the bread in to bake. The resulting steam creates a beautifully glossy, crackly crust.
